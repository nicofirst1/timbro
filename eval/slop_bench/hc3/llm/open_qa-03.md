Early humans are believed to have evolved in Africa and then spread to other parts of the world. The oldest known fossils of Homo sapiens, our species, have been found in eastern Africa and date back to around 300,000 years ago. However, there is evidence that Homo erectus, a closely related species, lived in various parts of Africa, Europe, and Asia as early as 1.8 million years ago.\n\nAs early humans evolved and developed new technologies, they were able to migrate to new areas and adapt to different environments. They eventually spread to every continent on Earth, including the Americas, which were likely first colonized by humans around 15,000 years ago.\n\nThroughout their history, early humans lived in a variety of environments, including forests, grasslands, deserts, and coastal regions. They developed a range of tools and technologies to help them survive and thrive in these different environments, including the use of fire, the development of hunting weapons, and the creation of shelters.
